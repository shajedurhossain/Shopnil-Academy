The Vowels
স্বরবর্ণ
🔤 Script📖 Vocabulary
💡
Good to know
Bangla has 11 vowel letters (স্বরবর্ণ, shorobôrno). Each one shown here is its independent form — the way it's written at the very start of a word. In Chapter 2 you'll learn how these change shape into "kar" signs when they attach to a consonant.

🧠 Memory trick
The pairs অ/আ, ই/ঈ, উ/ঊ each represent a historical short/long vowel distinction. In modern pronunciation ই and ঈ sound identical (and so do উ and ঊ) — the difference is mostly in spelling, not sound. ঋ is the rarest of the eleven — it shows up almost only in words borrowed from Sanskrit.

🔤 Script📚 Grammar Notes
Independent Vowels vs. "Kar" Signs
FormBangla termUsage
Independentস্বাধীন রূপUsed at the start of a word — e.g. অজগর, আম
Dependent ("kar" sign)কারAttaches to a consonant — taught in Chapter 2
Pronunciation Notes
LetterRomanizationNote
অoSometimes sounds closer to "ô" as in "hot"; varies a little by word
ই / ঈi / eeNearly identical in modern pronunciation — the difference is mainly in spelling
উ / ঊu / ooNearly identical in modern pronunciation — the difference is mainly in spelling
ঋriRare — found almost only in Sanskrit-derived words (e.g. ঋষি, ঋতু)
ঐ / ঔoi / ouDiphthongs — two vowel sounds pronounced together as one glide
🌐
West Bengal variety
This course follows Standard Bangladeshi Bangla (শুদ্ধ ভাষা). In West Bengal, অ is often pronounced closer to "ô" — so আম sounds more like "ām" than "aam" — but the script and spelling stay exactly the same; only the pronunciation shifts a little.
