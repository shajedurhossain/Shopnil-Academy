Lesson 1
The Four Tones — Sounds
四声 · 语音
The Four Tones四声

Mandarin is a tonal language: the pitch you use while saying a syllable is part of its meaning, not just an accent. The syllable ma means four completely different things depending on its tone. Tap each card to hear the difference.

1st Tone
mā
high, flat
妈 — mom
Pitch stays high and level, like holding a musical note.
2nd Tone
má
rising
麻 — hemp / numb
Pitch rises, like asking “what?” in English.
3rd Tone
mǎ
dip, then rise
马 — horse
Pitch falls low, then curls back up — like a skeptical “hmm?”
4th Tone
mà
sharp fall
骂 — to scold
Pitch drops fast from high to low, like a firm “No!”
Neutral Tone
ma
short, light
吗 — question particle
No independent pitch of its own — quick and unstressed.
💡 Same sound, different word
Try the same pattern with bā: bā (八, eight) · bá (拔, to pull) · bǎ (靶, target) · bà (爸, dad).

Lesson 1
The Four Tones — Rules
四声 · 规则
How the Tone System Works

Standard Mandarin (Putonghua) has 4 main tones plus a neutral tone. Each tone is a specific pitch contour layered onto the syllable — the consonant and vowel sounds ("ma") stay the same, only the pitch pattern changes.

ToneMarkPitch contourEnglish analogy
1stā55 — high, flatHolding a note while singing
2ndá35 — risingAsking “Huh?”
3rdǎ214 — falls then risesA skeptical “Well…”
4thà51 — sharp fallA firm “Stop!”
Neutrala (no mark)short, light, no fixed pitchAn unstressed syllable, like the “a” in “sofa”

These numbers (55, 35, 214, 51) come from the Chao tone letters system: 5 is the highest pitch in your vocal range, 1 is the lowest. A tone like 214 means the pitch starts mid, drops to low, then rises to high.

⚠️ Why tones matter
Getting a tone wrong doesn’t just sound like an accent — it can change the word entirely. Asking “马?” (mǎ, horse?) instead of “吗?” (ma, a yes/no question particle) changes the whole sentence’s meaning. This is why tones are taught before any vocabulary in this course.

The Four Tones — Reading Aloud
四声 · 朗读
Read Aloud: A Classic Tone Sentence

This well-known teaching sentence uses the syllable “ma” four times, each with a different tone. Listen first, then record yourself reading it aloud.

妈妈骑马，马慢，妈妈骂马。
Māma qí mǎ, mǎ màn, māma mà mǎ.
“Mom rides a horse; the horse is slow; mom scolds the horse.”
